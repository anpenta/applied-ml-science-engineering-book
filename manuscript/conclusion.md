Writing this book has been an opportunity to bring together lessons from working on both the scientific and engineering sides of applied machine learning. Across the chapters, we’ve examined why particular technical decisions make sense and what assumptions they depend on.

Those assumptions connect the scientific and engineering decisions we have examined. An inspection limit can change which model we prefer, and a more accurate component can make the system that depends on it perform worse. In both cases, an approach can be reasonable in one setting and unsuitable in another. Before reusing an approach that has worked for us previously, we should ask what made it work and whether those conditions hold in the new setting.

The same questions apply to the positions presented here. Readers may reach the end of this book disagreeing with some of the author’s positions, and that’s fine. If the book helps them think more critically about their own technical decisions, it will have served its purpose.
